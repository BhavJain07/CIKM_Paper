\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are often evaluated on whether an answer can be supported by \emph{some} retrieved passage.
Deployed assistants, however, must routinely choose among near-duplicate sources---mirrors, stale snapshots, scraped copies, and jurisdiction-specific variants---that share topical overlap but differ in authority, validity, and attribution.
We study this \emph{instance-level provenance} problem under lexical collision: many candidates look equally relevant, yet only one (or none) should be cited.

We present ProvBench-RAG, a benchmark specification and reproducible dataset format with clustered documents, gold preferred sources, minimal evidence sets, temporal and jurisdiction constraints, and abstention when authoritative sources disagree.
We further introduce PACER (Provenance-Aware Cluster Evidence Reranking), a transparent reranker that fuses min--max normalized BM25 scores with declarative provenance signals (authority, freshness, originality/copy labels, jurisdiction alignment) over a fixed candidate pool.

On a controlled synthetic split of 1{,}500 queries with explicit lexical decoys, BM25 achieves preferred-source accuracy at rank~1 (PSA@1) of 0 while PACER reaches 0.75 (McNemar $p<10^{-200}$, $n=1{,}200$), with composite ProvScore rising from 0.469 to 0.703 and nDCG@10 from 0.553 to 0.926.
Stratified analysis shows gains across freshness, authority, and jurisdiction families.
On 140 English Wikipedia revision clusters, PACER improves PSA@1 from 0.014 to 0.057 ($p=0.031$), though absolute accuracy remains low.
A pooled heterogeneous index (1{,}640 queries) shows recall collapse under a single lexical index (0.435) and partial recovery with provenance reranking (0.782).
All results regenerate from the released artifact.
\end{abstract}
